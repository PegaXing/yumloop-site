Multiplayer party brawler

Three choices.Eight players.Zero trust.

A fighting game with no attack button. You pick Rock, Paper or Scissors, then you become the weapon.

Rock Paper Rumble turns the oldest game in the playground into a physical multiplayer arena. Bluff your hand. Read your friends. Get into position. When RUMBLE! hits, chase what you beat and run from what beats you.

No combos. No moveset. Three choices and eight people lying to each other.

Then everyone hides again.

Rock, Paper or Scissors, changed as often as you like. Every change shows above your head for a second, so everyone is watching everyone.

Get close to what you think you beat. Stay away from whatever might beat you.

Every hand is revealed and locked at once. Now everybody knows who should chase and who should run.

Win the touch and they’re knocked out. Lose it and you are. The same hand? You bounce off each other.

Five seconds later the hands go hidden, the next countdown starts, and everyone remembers what they saw.

Every twenty seconds, a mind game turns into a chase.

Every cycle writes a story. The next one starts twenty seconds later.

Depth

Rock Paper Scissors isn’t the depth. The people are.

On paper it’s a coin with three sides. In an arena it becomes a game of memory, nerve and timing, because every choice is made in public and everyone is choosing at the same time.

The flashSwitch in plain sight so they pick the counter. Then switch again.
The late switchHold your real hand until the last possible second.
The poker faceYou saw their switch. Don’t turn toward them. Not yet.
MemoryFour people switched in the last five seconds. To what?
DistanceThe right hand in the wrong place is still the wrong hand.
The chainYou’re chasing someone. Someone’s chasing you. Someone else is chasing them.
The dashClose the gap in a blink. Or open one.
The three-second allianceTwo of you want the same Rumbler gone. Until the reveal ends.
The arenaA giant falling Rock, Paper or Scissors turns the safest route into the worst one.
Chaos with rules

The rules never change. The situation never stops changing.

Plenty of party games get their laughs from randomness. Rock Paper Rumble gets them from decisions. When someone gets launched, everyone watching knows exactly why, and exactly whose fault it was.

One rule everybody understands, applied by eight players at once, produces chaos that stays completely readable.

Party-game readabilityUnderstood from across the room within a round.

Seven ways to Rumble

Same three hands. Seven different arguments.

Every mode uses the same rule and the same controls. Each one changes what you’re fighting over. Every image is an in-game capture.

Four arenas

Four playgrounds. Every one has an edge.

Meet the Rumblers

Six Rumblers. Zero stat sheets.

Every Rumbler plays identically, and that’s deliberate. In a game about reading people, nobody should lose to a hidden number. You pick a Rumbler for personality, and so your friends can find you in a crowded arena.

The current Rumblers set the tone and proved the concept. Their final designs are part of the production plan; see art direction.

Local multiplayer

The couch is part of the design.

Up to four players on one PC, sharing one screen and one camera. No split screen: everyone sees the whole arena, including everyone else’s mistakes.

Online play runs on Epic Online Services: anonymous sign-in, no account to create, no port forwarding.

Quick PlayFinds a lobby with room for your whole couch, or opens one.
Room codesFour characters with no look-alike letters, easy to read out.
Game browserOpen lobbies with host names and free slots.
Direct joinBy code or IP address, with LAN when you want it.
Host controlsThe host sets mode, arena, bot count and bot difficulty.
Bots fill the gapsA lobby of three still plays as eight.
Drop-in, drop-outLeavers are replaced by bots. New players take a bot’s place.
Vote, then go againA mode vote, an arena vote, then straight into the next match together.
Couch + onlineUp to eight humans, from any mix of machines.

Today’s online play is player-hosted and peer-to-peer. There are no dedicated servers, matchmaking or host migration yet: choosing the right architecture for launch is part of the production plan.

Bots

They know what you showed them. Not what you’re hiding.

Bots never peek at hidden hands. They play from the same information a person has: the flashes they caught, the reveals they remember and where everyone is standing.

  • Remember flashes, and trust old ones less
  • Counter what they remember
  • Bluff, and switch late
  • Chase what they beat, flee what beats them
  • Dash to close a gap or escape one
  • Step out of hazards and hostile floor
  • Play the objective in every mode
  • Go after Shields, Jammers and Wild Cards
  • Jump the gaps between platforms

Art direction

The game is proven. Its final look is still open.

To prove the design quickly, the current build uses AI-assisted placeholder art in places, across characters, UI illustration and environments. That was the right call for a prototype. It isn’t the plan for a commercial release.

The commercial version gets a cohesive, original visual identity made by professional artists. The six Rumblers and today’s tone are a starting point, not sacred production assets.
