Report a bug

Crashes, a level that won't load, a button that does nothing? Send it over. Three things make it far faster to fix, and they're all in one place: Settings → General → About on your phone, and the version number at the bottom of the game's own settings screen.

  • What happened, and what you were doing right before it.
  • Your device and iOS version.
  • The app version.

A screenshot or screen recording is worth a lot here, especially for anything visual.

Report a bug

A purchase didn't go through

First try Restore Purchases in the game's settings. That re-checks your receipt with Apple and is the fix in most cases. It has to be the same Apple ID you bought with.

If that doesn't work, email me and I'll sort it out. For a refund, Apple handles billing directly at reportaproblem.apple.com. I can't issue refunds myself, but tell me anyway so I can fix whatever caused it.

I lost my progress

Progress is saved on your device and backed up automatically to your own iCloud, so it follows your Apple ID. Reinstall the app, or open the game on a new iPhone or iPad signed into the same Apple ID, and your progress comes back by itself — give it a few seconds on the main menu with an internet connection. Syncing only ever moves you forward: an old device with an old save can never roll a newer one back.

If progress still seems lost after that, check the device is signed into iCloud under Settings → [your name] and that iCloud Drive is on. And if progress vanished without any of the above, that is a bug and I want to hear about it.

Moving progress to another device or platform

Sightline can carry your whole progress between an iPhone, an Android phone and Steam with a save code. On the device that has your progress, open Settings → Transfer progress and tap Export: you get a short code such as SL-DNMQ8L9BE. On the other device, open the same screen, type the code and tap Import. The code holds your levels, stars, streaks, statistics and settings, and stays valid for 30 days.

Importing never quietly throws progress away: if the device you're importing to is already further along, the game tells you and asks before replacing anything. Without an internet connection the export screen offers a long code you can copy and paste instead.

Steam and Steam Deck

The Steam version is the complete game, with no in-app purchases. It supports mouse, keyboard and controllers, and plays fully on Steam Deck without a touchscreen: the d-pad or left stick moves a tile highlight, A reveals, X flags, B goes back, Y opens the Glossary, the triggers zoom the board and the left stick pans it. Every control can be changed under Settings → Controls, which also lists the keyboard shortcuts. Friends and leaderboards use your Steam friends list, and your save is backed up to Steam Cloud.

I'm stuck on a puzzle

Every puzzle in a Yumloop game is solvable by deduction alone. You never have to guess, and if a board seems to require guessing, that's a bug worth reporting. Send me the level number and I'll either walk you through the next step or fix the level.

Game Center, leaderboards and friends

On iPhone and iPad, leaderboards, friends and achievements run on Apple's Game Center (on Steam they run on Steam), and they're available to every player — nothing social sits behind a purchase. If yours aren't showing, check you're signed in under Settings → Game Center, and for friends' times, allow the game to connect with friends when iOS asks. Signing in is optional. Everything in the game is playable without it.

Accessibility and customisation

Sightline never relies on colour alone — every mechanic has its own shape. On top of that, the game's settings include colour blind presets for deuteranopia, protanopia and tritanopia, and a Custom colours editor that hands you a dial for every recolourable element: bombs, flags, the sightline itself, portals, arrows, and each number from 1 to 5.

There's also dark mode, a reduced motion switch that calms the animations, and an optional on-screen flag button if long-pressing isn't comfortable. If something about the game is hard to see or hard to reach, tell me — accessibility reports go to the top of the list.

Music, sound and haptics

Each has its own switch and volume in the game's settings, and they're independent: turning the music off leaves the sound effects playing, and turning sounds off leaves the music alone. The game also steps aside automatically if you're listening to your own music or a podcast.
