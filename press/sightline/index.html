About the game

One rule. Read it in seconds.

What if Minesweeper stopped looking next door and started looking across the whole board?

Sightline is a deduction puzzle built on one rule you can read in seconds. A revealed tile shows numbers on its edges, and each number counts the bombs it can see in that direction: the whole line, until something blocks it. Combine what the clues can see and the board tells you exactly which tiles are safe.

Then the board starts bending the rules. Walls block a sightline. Channels let it through one way and block it across. Mirrors turn it 90 degrees, sometimes around the whole board. One-way arrows, holes and purple neighbour tiles change what each number means, and linked portals carry a sightline somewhere a straight line could never reach. The board changes. The rule doesn't.

Every board is checked by a solver before it ships, so there is always a logical next move. If it ever feels like a guess, there's a clue you haven't read yet.

Sightline has 400 campaign levels across eight board shapes, 300 Grand Levels (large boards drawn as pictures), a Time Challenge, a Daily puzzle that is the same for everyone in the world, and live cross-platform Elimination multiplayer for up to 16 players. It is coming to Steam (Windows, macOS, Steam Deck), iPhone and iPad, and Android.

No guessing. No luck. Just one more level.

What makes it different
Clues look along lines, not at neighboursIn most grid deduction games a number describes the tiles touching it. In Sightline a number describes everything it can see in one direction, so one clue can reach across the whole board and two clues far apart can solve each other.
The board changes how you seeNew mechanics don't add new rules. They change the path a sightline takes: blocked, bent, filtered or teleported. The same single rule keeps producing new kinds of deduction.
Never a coin flipEvery level, Daily and multiplayer board is verified solvable by pure logic before it reaches the player. Late-game Minesweeper-style 50/50 guesses don't exist.
Logic as a raceMultiplayer turns deduction into a live knock-out: everyone solves the same boards at once, and one bomb ends your match.
Mechanics, in teaching order
Directional clues Level 1
A number sits on one edge of its tile. That edge is the direction it looks; the number is how many bombs lie along that whole line.
× tiles Level 3
A tile that sees no bombs in any direction: every tile in line with it is safe.
Walls Level 5
Sightlines stop dead at a wall and can't see past it.
Flags Level 7
Optional markers for where you think a bomb is. Purely for keeping track.
Channels Level 9
A window: sight passes through along its arrows in either direction, and is blocked across them.
Purple tiles Level 12
Ignore sightlines entirely. The number counts bombs in the eight touching tiles, corners included, and other sightlines pass straight through them.
Mirrors Level 25
Bend a sightline 90 degrees. The number counts bombs along the whole bent path; several mirrors can curve one sightline around the board.
Holes Level 48
Gaps inside the board. Sightlines stop at a hole just as they do at a wall.
One-way arrows Level 50
Let a sightline pass only the way the arrow points. Every other direction is blocked like a wall.
Portals Level 75
A linked pair. A sightline entering one leaves from the other, reaching bombs a straight line can't.
Modes & features
Campaign
400 levels across eight board shapes (square, rectangle, diamond, triangle, circle, plus, heart and islands). Each mechanic is introduced in its own lesson level, and checkpoints jump straight to any of them.
Grand Levels
A second campaign of 300 large boards drawn as pictures (mazes, castles, islands, constellations), made for big screens.
Daily Sightline
One new board a day, the same for every player in the world, with streaks and a global leaderboard.
Time Challenge
30 boards against the clock, unlocked through the campaign.
Multiplayer: Elimination
Up to 16 players solve the same boards at the same time. Hit a bomb and you're out; the last player standing wins. Quick Play, Ranked, and private rooms with a five-letter code. Cross-platform across iPhone, iPad, Android, Windows, Mac and Steam Deck.
One save everywhere
Progress syncs through the player's platform account (Game Center, Google Play Games, Steam), and a one-time link code joins different ecosystems.
Built for every input
Touch, mouse, keyboard and controller, including Steam Deck; colour-blind palettes and reduced motion.

Developer

Yumloop

Yumloop is a new independent games studio founded by Yoni, a game designer who previously worked at Rockstar North on Grand Theft Auto V, Red Dead Redemption 2 and Grand Theft Auto VI.

Sightline is Yumloop's first game, designed and built solo. It started with one question: what if a Minesweeper-style clue didn't tell you what was immediately around it, but what it could see?

“I love games where complexity emerges from a small number of consistent rules rather than continually adding exceptions, and Sightline grew naturally from that idea.” (Yoni, designer)
